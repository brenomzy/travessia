## Tension

> Getting paid from abroad is one crossing split between a client's card, a bank wire, an exchange desk and an accountant, and the freelancer is the only one who sees all of it, usually after up to 6% has gone.

The problem isn't only the price. Nobody owns the whole crossing, so the freelancer carries it: chasing the wire, guessing the spread, issuing the tax invoice by hand at month end. Travessia owns it end to end: the invoice, the conversion, the Pix and the tax invoice in one place. The "up to 6%" is SPEC §4.2's range for wires and bad rates.

## Promise

> **Paid abroad. Sorted at home.**

- **What it means.** The client pays from abroad; everything that has to happen in Brazil (reais by Pix, the export tax invoice, the month's tax report) is done on this side.
- **Where it goes.** It closes every page, deck and campaign, the way Ramp's "Time is money. Save both." does. It is the sign-off, not the headline.
- **What it pairs with.** The home hero stays the product line: "Your client pays in dollars. You get paid in reais." The promise says why that matters.
- **The river.** The line doesn't use the crossing image; the name, the seam, the colour names and the photography carry it. Copy can still use the crossing words that step 4 allows.
- **The test.** Read it next to any page on the site. If the page doesn't show something being paid abroad or sorted at home, either the page or the line is wrong.

## Category

**An account for getting paid from abroad, for Brazilian freelancers and studios.**

It matches how the audience searches ("how to get paid from abroad as a Brazilian freelancer", "receber do exterior") and describes the job rather than the product type. Use "account" on its own once the context is set; avoid "international account", "cross-border account" and "platform" in marketing copy. If the category widens later, the next step is the studio's whole back office for foreign clients.

## Beliefs

Things Travessia believes that a bank wouldn't say.

1. **The exchange rate is part of the price.** A fee you can't see before you agree to it is still a fee, so the rate and the fee come before the result, every time.
2. **A freelancer billing London is an exporter.** Exporting a service comes with an exporter's paperwork, and that paperwork should be done for them, not left for month end.
3. **Your money shouldn't wait for us.** We hold it only as long as the conversion takes.
4. **Taxes are a task, not a threat.** Explain the term, show the number, skip the warning.
5. **The client is part of the deal.** An invoice that looks amateur or is hard to pay costs the freelancer, so the client's side gets the same care.

## Will and won't say

**Will say:**
- Exact numbers, each with its source in `brand/proof.md` (fictional, labelled).
- The fee and the rate before the amount that arrives.
- Portuguese terms only where English has no real equivalent. Pix stays (a proper name). MEI and carnê-leão stay, each with a short explanation the first time. Nota fiscal becomes **tax invoice**: written "tax invoice (nota fiscal)" on its first mention on a page, then "tax invoice". The client's document is always just "invoice".
- The crossing, sparingly: a small set of words (cross, the other side, arrive) with a limit set in step 4.
- What Travessia doesn't do (credit, investments, cards), as the About page already does.

**Won't say:**
- Tax scare copy ("the Receita is coming", fines as a hook).
- "Hidden fees" outrage, or swipes at named banks.
- The SPEC §4.3 list: solution, seamless, leverage, revolutionary, empower.
- "Borderless", "without borders", "sem fronteiras".
- "Instant" or "in seconds". Pix lands in 1 business day; say that.
- Anything that sounds like tax advice. Travessia prepares the report; the accountant advises.
- Brazil as a postcard: samba, carnival, beaches as shorthand for Brazilian.

## Open item

Company size conflicts between sources: SPEC §4.1 says Series A, about 40 people; the About page says 14 people in São Paulo and Recife, serving 2,140 accounts. Settle it in step 5 (proof), where every number gets one source.

## Options considered in round 1

Kept so later steps can see what was turned down and why.

### Tension

- **A. One crossing, nobody owns it.** *Chosen.*
- **B. The surprise.** At a Brazilian bank, the exchange rate is something you find out after the money arrives: a US$ 4,000 payment can land R$ 600 short of that day's rate. Concrete (it's the About page story), but a price complaint, which is the ground Wise and Nubank stand on. Still good material for the About page and for proof.
- **C. Two systems.** The client pays in one country's system and the freelancer is taxed in another's, so every foreign invoice turns into a wire, a spread and a nota fiscal issued by hand at month end. Closest to the paperwork angle, with a weaker number.

### The three promises put forward

| Line | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid abroad. Sorted at home.** *Chosen.* | Covers both sides, including the tax side nobody else claims. Short enough for a billboard. | No river image. "Sorted" leans British. |
| Know the fare before you cross. | Carries the crossing and the fee-first promise in five words. | About price only, the most crowded promise in the category; says nothing about the nota fiscal. |
| Home and dry. | An idiom that already means "safely across the water and finished". | Better known in British English; needs a subhead to say what is home and dry. |

### Category word

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| An account for getting paid from abroad | *Chosen.* |
| An international account | What the footer says today; generic, and Wise's home H1 is "The international account…". |
| A cross-border account | SPEC's wording; accurate, but nobody searches for it, and it sounds like B2B payments. |
| An export account | True in the tax sense, but most freelancers don't think of themselves as exporters yet. Belief 2 makes that case instead. |

### Promise candidates

Thirty lines in five directions. **Bold** marks the three that were put forward; 7 was chosen. Notes say what's wrong where something is.

#### The fare before you board (price known before the crossing)

1. **Know the fare before you cross.**
2. A short crossing, priced before you board.
3. See the price of the crossing before you take it. *(Too long to close a page.)*
4. The rate first. Then the reais.
5. No surprises on the other side. *("No surprises" is common fintech copy.)*
6. You know what arrives before it leaves.

#### The whole crossing (both sides, invoice to nota fiscal)

7. **Paid abroad. Sorted at home.**
8. Paid in dollars. Filed in reais.
9. From invoice to nota fiscal, one crossing.
10. Everything between the invoice and the Pix. *(Already a Home section title; better there.)*
11. Both sides of every payment.
12. Work abroad. Get paid at home.
13. Clients abroad. Taxes at home. One account.

#### The money arrives (what lands on the other side)

14. Nothing lost in the crossing. *(Not literally true: the 0.6% fee is lost, on purpose and shown.)*
15. Nothing lost in translation. Or in conversion. *(Same problem; the translation half is good for translators.)*
16. The other side, by tomorrow.
17. Your work crosses. So should your money.
18. It arrives as reais, with the paperwork done.

#### Known phrases, bent (Ramp's method)

19. **Home and dry.**
20. Crossed and cleared. *(From banking: a crossed cheque, "cheque cruzado", is familiar to Brazilians; "cleared" is what a payment does. Possibly too insider.)*
21. Worth the crossing. *(From "worth the trip". Vague about what's worth it.)*
22. Cross the river, not your fingers. *(Too jokey for the calm register.)*
23. The long way, made short.
24. Get paid, then get on with it.

#### Plain and functional (the current voice)

25. Your client pays in dollars. You get paid in reais. *(Current hero. Better as the product line than the promise.)*
26. Reais by Pix, one business day after your client pays.
27. One fee, shown first. Reais the next day. Nota fiscal done.
28. Get paid from abroad the way you get paid at home.
29. Clients anywhere. Reais here.
30. Foreign clients, Brazilian paperwork, one account.
