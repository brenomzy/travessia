## Who has to pay

If you're paid by a client abroad as an individual, with no company in Brazil, that money goes into the carnê-leão, Brazil's monthly income tax for individuals. It's worked out every month: each month has its own calculation and its own DARF payment slip.

If you invoice through an MEI or a Simples company, you don't use the carnê-leão. The tax goes through the company, and the export nota fiscal replaces this calculation.

<aside>Travessia shows the value in reais of every payment on the day it lands. You export the whole month as a CSV for the carnê-leão.</aside>

## How to convert each payment

Each payment is converted at the dollar buying rate of the last business day of the first half of the previous month. It sounds odd, but that's the rule: the rate isn't the one from the day the money arrived.

Example values. Check the month's official rate with your accountant.

## Deadlines and the DARF

Each month's DARF is due on the last business day of the following month. Paid in January, you pay by the end of February. Late payments carry a fine and interest.

1. Log each payment in the carnê-leão app.
2. Add the month's deductible expenses, if any.
3. Generate the DARF and pay it by the due date.

## What to keep for the annual return

Keep the invoices, the payment receipts and the DARFs you paid. On the annual return, the carnê-leão values come in automatically when you import the year's data.
